Pricing by hand

What goes into the price of your home?

  • The home itself. Condition, updates, layout, square footage, lot and the systems buyers will inspect: roof, HVAC, water heater, windows.
  • Recent comparable sales. Closed sales nearby, adjusted for real differences in size, age, finish and setting.
  • Your competition. The homes a buyer will tour the same weekend as yours, and how long similar homes are taking to sell.
  • The street and the setting. Traffic, views, water, flood zone, HOA, and whether the home is inside city limits.
  • Your timing. What preparation could change, and what the calendar means for buyer activity.

Ally’s construction background helps here. She can tell which updates buyers will pay for and which ones they will discount, and what a needed repair is likely to cost.

The process

How does a pricing consultation work?

  1. Request a time

    Use the form on this page or call the Belmont office. Share the address and your rough timing.

  2. Ally studies the market first

    Before she visits, Ally reviews recent nearby sales, active listings and homes that did not sell.

  3. The walk-through

    She walks the home with you, inside and out, and asks about updates, repairs and anything a buyer will ask about.

  4. Your pricing conversation

    Ally explains the range she would list in, the sales behind it, and which repairs or staging could change it.

  5. Your plan, if you want one

    If you decide to sell, she builds a week-by-week plan with the selling timeline. If not, you keep the information.

Automated estimates

Why not just use an online estimate?

Online estimates are useful for curiosity and useless for a list price. They cannot see that your kitchen was redone last year or that the house next door needs a roof. They often lag in fast-changing neighborhoods and struggle with unusual homes: acreage, lake frontage, custom construction and historic houses.

Pricing too high costs time on the market, and buyers notice. Pricing too low leaves money on the table. A price Ally can explain is one you can defend when an offer or an appraisal comes in.

Straight answers

Home value questions

Is a pricing consultation the same as an appraisal?

No. A pricing consultation is Ally’s professional market opinion of where your home should be listed. A formal appraisal is ordered by a lender and completed by a licensed appraiser, usually after you accept an offer. Ally prices with the appraisal in mind, because a contract price the appraisal cannot support can create problems for the buyer’s loan.

Why do online home value estimates differ so much?

Automated estimates work from public records and recent sales. They cannot see your updates, your layout, the condition of the roof or the street your home sits on, and different websites use different formulas. Ally uses them only as a starting point for questions, never as the price.

Is my Gaston County tax value my market value?

Not necessarily. Gaston County last reappraised property in 2023 and schedules its next reappraisal for 2027, so assessed values can lag behind the market in either direction. Buyers and appraisers look at recent sales, not the tax card.

Does a pricing consultation cost anything?

No. There is no charge and no obligation to list with Ally. If you decide to sell, she will explain the listing agreement and her compensation in writing before you sign anything.

How soon before selling should I ask for a pricing consultation?

Ideally six to eight weeks before you want to list. That leaves time for the repairs, staging and documents the conversation usually turns up. The selling timeline tool works backward from your list date.
