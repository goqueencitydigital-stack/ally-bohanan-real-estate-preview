Top 5 Curb Appeal Wins Under $500 in North Carolina

Five low-cost exterior projects that make a North Carolina home look cared for in photos and at the curb, plus what to skip.

The short answer

The best low-cost curb appeal wins for North Carolina sellers are a thorough cleanup and pressure wash, fresh mulch or pine straw with neat edging, a clean or freshly painted front door with updated hardware and house numbers, working exterior lights, and a tidy, simply staged porch. Together they make a home look well cared for before buyers walk inside.

Key takeaways

  1. Cleaning and pressure washing often make the biggest difference for the least money.
  2. Crisp bed edges and fresh mulch or pine straw make any yard look kept.
  3. The front door, hardware and house numbers are what buyers see up close.
  4. Working, matching exterior lights matter for evening showings and twilight photos.
  5. Do the work in the right order: clean, repair, then refresh.

You don't need a big landscaping project to improve how your home looks from the street. These five projects focus on what buyers notice in the first photo and the first few seconds at the curb. Costs vary with your home's size and condition, and whether you do the work yourself, but each can be done on a modest budget.

1. Clean everything

North Carolina's pollen, humidity and storms leave siding, walkways and roofs looking dingy quickly. Start here.

  • Pressure wash the driveway, walkways, porch and siding. Use a gentle wash on painted or delicate surfaces.
  • Clear the gutters and remove debris from the roof.
  • Wash the exterior windows and light fixtures.
  • Remove clutter: hoses, bins, toys, tools and dead plants.

2. Edge the beds and refresh the mulch or pine straw

  • Cut clean edges along the beds and walkways.
  • Pull weeds, and trim shrubs back from the windows and walkways.
  • Spread fresh mulch or pine straw.
  • Add a few healthy plants by the entry. NC State Extension's plant toolbox can help you choose plants suited to the sun and season.

3. Update the front door

The front door is where buyers stand while their agent opens the lockbox. Make it look cared for:

  • Clean it or repaint it in a color that suits the house. Check HOA rules if they apply.
  • Replace a worn handle and lock set.
  • Install clear, updated house numbers.
  • Replace a cracked doorbell or a dated light next to the door.
  • Add a new doormat.

4. Fix and match the exterior lighting

  • Replace any burned-out bulbs, using the same color throughout.
  • Clean or replace worn fixtures by the door and garage.
  • Add a few path or landscape lights if the walkway is dark.

Good lighting matters for evening showings in winter and for twilight photos.

5. Stage the porch

  • Use one or two clean chairs or a small bench.
  • Add a pair of matching planters.
  • Remove worn cushions, extra decor and storage items.
  • Touch up the paint on the railings, posts and steps.

What order should you do it in?

StepWhy
1. CleanShows what actually needs repair or paint
2. RepairTakes care of loose boards, gutters and broken fixtures
3. Paint and updateDoor, trim, hardware and lighting
4. LandscapeEdging, mulch and plants last, so they stay fresh
5. StageThe porch and entry, right before photos

Tip: Take a photo of your house from across the street, the way a listing photographer would. Anything that catches your eye on the phone screen goes on the list.

What should you skip?

Big hardscape projects, mature trees and full exterior repaints rarely make sense right before a sale unless the existing condition is a real problem. For more ideas, see our outdoor living upgrades and online listing tips.

Straight answers

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest curb appeal upgrade?

Cleaning. Pressure washing, clearing gutters, washing windows and removing clutter cost mostly time, and they change how the whole exterior looks.

What color should I paint my front door?

Choose something that suits the house and the neighborhood. Classic dark colors and muted tones are safe choices. If you have an HOA, check its rules first.

Should I plant flowers before listing?

A few healthy plants in pots or beds by the entry help. Choose varieties suited to the season and your sun exposure. NC State Extension's plant toolbox is a good reference.

When should I do curb appeal work?

Before the listing photos. The front exterior is usually the lead photo, so it needs to be finished before the photographer comes.
