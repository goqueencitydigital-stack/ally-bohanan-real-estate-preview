What to Expect From a Home Appraisal in Lowell, NC

How appraisals work on Lowell purchases, what the appraiser looks at, how to prepare, and your options if the value comes in low.

The short answer

When a Lowell buyer finances a purchase, the lender orders an appraisal from a licensed appraiser, who visits the home, compares it with recent nearby sales, and reports a market value. If it comes in low, buyers and sellers can renegotiate, cover the gap, request a reconsideration, or in some cases terminate.

Key takeaways

  1. The lender orders the appraisal, and the appraiser works independently of the agents and the parties.
  2. Comparable sales, condition, size, layout and location drive the value.
  3. Sellers can help by having the home clean, accessible and documented with a list of updates.
  4. A low appraisal is a negotiation point, not automatically the end of the deal.
  5. In North Carolina, plan the appraisal inside the due diligence period when possible.

Lowell's homes vary from older houses on established streets to newer subdivisions, so finding truly comparable sales can take care. Here's how the appraisal fits into a purchase, and what both buyers and sellers can do.

What is an appraisal, and who orders it?

An appraisal is a licensed appraiser's independent opinion of a home's market value. On a financed purchase, the lender orders it to make sure the home supports the loan, as the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains. The buyer typically pays for it. North Carolina appraisers are licensed through the NC Appraisal Board. Cash buyers can order their own appraisal for peace of mind.

What does the appraiser look at?

FactorExamples
Comparable salesRecent nearby sales of similar size, age, style and lot
Size and layoutFinished living area, bedrooms, bathrooms, garage
Condition and qualityWear, deferred maintenance, quality of finishes
UpdatesKitchen, baths, roof, HVAC and their age
SiteLot size, usability, views, outbuildings, flood zone
Market conditionsWhether values in the area are rising, stable or falling

Appraisers adjust each comparable up or down for differences from your home. Finished square footage is measured under specific standards, so a bonus room or basement may be counted differently than you expect.

How can sellers prepare?

  • Make the home clean and easy to access, including attic, crawlspace and mechanical areas.
  • Fix obvious issues such as leaks, missing trim or broken fixtures.
  • Prepare a one-page list of updates with dates and approximate costs.
  • Have permits for additions or finished spaces available.
  • Let your agent share relevant comparable sales through the proper channels.

What should buyers know?

  • Timing: in North Carolina, aim to have the appraisal done before your due diligence period ends, so you still have options if it's low.
  • Your loan type matters. FHA and VA appraisals include property condition requirements.
  • You're entitled to a copy of the appraisal from your lender.

Read our step-by-step buying guide for how due diligence works.

What if the appraisal comes in low?

  1. Review the report. Check for factual errors, such as wrong square footage or missed features.
  2. Request a reconsideration of value through the lender if there are errors or better comparables.
  3. Renegotiate the price, or split the difference.
  4. Cover the gap in cash if the buyer can and wants to.
  5. Change the loan structure, such as a larger down payment, with the lender's guidance.
  6. Terminate, if you're still within due diligence or your contract allows it.

Is an appraisal the same as a pricing consultation?

No. An appraisal is a formal valuation for a lender. A pricing consultation is your agent's recommendation on list price based on competition and buyer demand. Both rely on comparables, but they serve different purposes. Read what affects resale value most in Lowell for the factors behind both.

Frequently asked questions

Does a home inspection replace an appraisal?

No. An inspection evaluates condition for the buyer, while an appraisal estimates value for the lender. Most financed buyers need both.

Can a seller be present during the appraisal?

Usually sellers aren't required to be there, and many leave so the appraiser can work. Your agent can arrange access and share a list of updates.

Can an appraisal be waived?

Some loans qualify for an appraisal waiver, depending on the lender and loan program. Ask your lender, and consider whether you'd want one anyway for your own protection.
