Talk With a Buyer’s Agent

The short answer

A buyer’s agent represents you, not the seller, when you buy a home. In Gastonia and the rest of Gaston County, Ally Bohanan helps buyers set a budget with a lender, narrow the right towns, judge each house’s condition with a builder’s eye, write and negotiate the offer, and manage due diligence through closing. North Carolina requires a written buyer agency agreement before any offer is made. Ally is licensed in North Carolina and South Carolina.

Updated November 2026

Buyer representation

What does a buyer’s agent do for you?

A buyer’s agent turns a broad home search into a series of clear decisions. Ally helps you define what you actually need, compares each property on its own facts, prepares offer terms you understand, and keeps every deadline organized after the seller says yes.

  • Search strategy built around how you want to live, not just price and bedrooms.
  • Showings with honest notes on condition, layout, lot and resale.
  • Pricing context from recent nearby sales before you write an offer.
  • Negotiation on price, due diligence fee, earnest money, repairs and closing date.
  • Coordination with your lender, inspector, insurer and closing attorney.

Good comparison goes beyond photos and asking price. Disclosures, restrictions, recurring costs and access can change from one address to the next. Ally helps you verify them through listing documents, public records and the right professionals.

The difference

Why does a builder’s eye matter on a showing?

Ally is also the business manager and designer at Bohanan Construction, which builds custom homes in the area. That background changes what she notices in a house: the roofline and drainage, the age of the mechanical systems, the quality of the windows and trim, and which walls could move in a renovation.

It does not replace a licensed home inspector. It helps you decide which houses deserve an inspection in the first place, and what the inspector should look at most closely. Clients have noticed. Jennifer Grant’s review mentions Ally’s knowledge of renovation and construction as a reason she trusted the advice.

The buying process

How does buying a home in North Carolina work?

Most North Carolina purchases follow the same six steps. The order matters, especially the lender conversation before you fall for a house.

  1. Talk with a lender

    A pre-approval tells you what you can comfortably spend and makes your offer stronger. Ally works with a preferred local lender, and you are always free to choose your own. Use the mortgage calculator to test rough numbers first.

  2. Sign a buyer agency agreement

    North Carolina requires it in writing before any offer. It sets out Ally’s duties, the length of the agreement and her compensation. You will also review the state’s Working With Real Estate Agents disclosure at your first substantial meeting.

  3. Narrow the towns, then tour

    Start with Find My Carolina Community and the town guides, then tour the homes that fit. Ally sends her current listings and new matches as they appear.

  4. Write the offer

    Price is one term among many. Ally helps you set the due diligence fee, earnest money, due diligence period and closing date so the offer is strong without giving away protection you need.

  5. Use due diligence well

    This is your window to inspect, appraise, review insurance, check flood maps, read HOA documents and confirm the survey. You can terminate for any reason during this period, so Ally keeps every task on a calendar.

  6. Close and get keys

    North Carolina closings are usually handled by a closing attorney. Ally stays involved through the final walk-through and the signing, and she is still a call away after you move in.

Due diligence

What should you check before making an offer?

  • County and city limits. Taxes and services change at the line. Gaston County’s rate is 59.9 cents per $100 of assessed value as of July 1, 2026, and each city adds its own. In South Carolina, a primary residence is assessed at 4 percent instead of 6 percent once you apply with the county.
  • Flood zone. Homes near the Catawba River, the South Fork and their creeks can sit in mapped flood areas. Look up the address on the NC Flood Risk Information System and get an insurance quote early.
  • Well and septic. Common in western Gaston County and outside town limits. Budget for inspections of both.
  • HOA and restrictions. Planned communities can limit fences, rentals, boats and exterior changes. Read the documents during due diligence.
  • Seller disclosures. North Carolina sellers must give you the Residential Property and Owners’ Association Disclosure Statement. “No representation” is a legal answer, so ask follow-up questions.

Straight answers

Buyer questions, answered

Do I need a written agreement to work with a buyer’s agent in North Carolina?

Yes. The North Carolina Real Estate Commission requires a written buyer agency agreement before a buyer’s agent presents any offer for you, written or oral. The agreement spells out the agent’s duties, how long it lasts and how the agent is paid. Ally reviews it with you line by line before you sign.

Who pays the buyer’s agent?

It depends on what you negotiate. Under NCREC rules, the buyer agency agreement must state the compensation and who pays it. Sometimes a seller offers to cover some or all of it, and sometimes the buyer does. Ask Ally to walk through the options before you sign anything.

What is a due diligence fee, and is it refundable?

In a North Carolina purchase, the due diligence fee is a negotiated amount paid directly to the seller for the right to inspect and to walk away for any reason during the due diligence period. It is generally not refundable unless the seller breaches the contract, and it is credited to you at closing. Earnest money is separate. It is held in escrow and returned if you terminate within the due diligence period.

Does Ally help buyers in South Carolina?

Yes. Ally is licensed in both North Carolina and South Carolina. Her Zillow profile shows 14 recorded South Carolina sales as of November 19, 2026, in York County towns such as Clover, Lake Wylie, Rock Hill, York and Fort Mill. Contracts, taxes and closing customs differ across the state line, so she explains each one as it comes up.

Can I buy a home before I sell mine?

Often, yes, but it depends on your financing. Talk with a lender first about whether you can carry two payments or need your sale to close first. Ally can then plan the timing of both sides and write terms that protect you.

How long does it take to buy a home in Gastonia?

Searches vary from a few days to many months. Once you are under contract, the timeline is set by the due diligence period and the closing date you and the seller agree on. Debbie Searcy’s review describes finding her home within days and closing within two weeks, which is fast. Most buyers should plan for longer.
