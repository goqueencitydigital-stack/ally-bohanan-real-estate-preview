Should I buy or build a home?

Six questions about land, timing, location and how involved you want to be. You get a side-by-side brief: what favors buying, what favors building, and what to find out next.

The short answer

Buying usually fits a firm move date, an established neighborhood and a wish to skip design decisions. Building usually fits owning or wanting specific land, must-haves resale rarely offers, and flexible timing. This tool weighs your answers and tells you when both paths are reasonable.

            How the brief works

            Six questions, each tied to a reason

            Every line in your brief points back to one of your answers, so you can see why a path came out ahead. When the reasons are close, the brief says that both paths are reasonable rather than forcing a choice.

            Owning land changes the questions. If you already have a lot, the brief adds the site questions to settle first: access, utilities, soil and septic, restrictions and a site evaluation. If you have a firm move date, it lists the timing questions to ask each team.

            The case for buying

            When buying an existing home makes sense

            You can walk through the house, see the light and the street, and know what you are getting. That matters most when you have a firm move date, when the neighborhood comes first, or when you would rather not make hundreds of design decisions.

            Established neighborhoods also come with mature trees, known schools and settled streets. If you are flexible about renovating, the right existing home can be updated over time. Ally’s buyer representation starts with what you want your days to look like, and the community guides help you compare towns.

            The case for building

            When building a custom home makes sense

            Sometimes the house you want does not exist yet. A main-floor primary suite, a specific layout, a shop, acreage or a covered porch big enough for the whole family can be hard to find on the resale market, and building lets you decide each one.

            Owning land, or wanting a particular kind of land, often tips the balance. Building asks more of you: time, patience and many decisions. Bohanan Construction guides those decisions, and finding land and building on your lot explains what to check first.

            When both paths are reasonable

            Many people land in the middle: some flexibility on timing, a few must-haves, and no land yet. In that case it helps to do both kinds of homework at once. Ask Ally what homes with your must-haves have been selling for and how quickly, and ask Bohanan Construction what it would take to build them, starting with land. Compare the answers side by side.

            A nearly new home can be a middle path too. Ally’s tour of a recently built Gastonia home shows what makes a nearly new house still feel new.

            Straight answers

            Buy or build questions

            Is it better to buy or build a home in Gaston County?

            Neither is better for everyone. Buying usually suits a firm move date, a specific established neighborhood and a wish to skip design decisions. Building usually suits people who own land or want specific land, have must-haves the resale market rarely offers, and have room in their timing. Many people find that both are reasonable.

            Do I need land before I can think about building?

            No. Many people decide to build first and then look for land. Ally is a licensed real estate broker and can help you search and evaluate land with a build in mind. See finding land.

            Does this tool tell me what a build would cost or how long it would take?

            No. It is educational only. Cost and timing depend on the land, the plan, permitting, selections and weather, so it does not give prices, feasibility or completion dates. The brief lists the questions to ask instead.

            What happens to my answers?

            They stay in your browser. Nothing is sent unless you choose one of the two actions and submit the form, which includes a summary of your brief.
