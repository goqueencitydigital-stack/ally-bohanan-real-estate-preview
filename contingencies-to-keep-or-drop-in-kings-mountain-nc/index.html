Contingencies to Keep or Drop in Kings Mountain, NC: How Buyer Protections Work in North Carolina

North Carolina handles buyer protections differently from most states. Here's what you keep or give up in a Kings Mountain offer.

By Ally BohananPublished May 16, 2026Updated November 16, 20263 min read

The short answer

The standard North Carolina contract protects buyers mainly through the due diligence period. During it, you can inspect, appraise, finalize your loan and walk away for any reason, losing only the due diligence fee. A shorter period or larger fee strengthens an offer. Keep enough time to inspect older Kings Mountain homes, and add other contingencies only when needed.

Key takeaways

  1. In North Carolina, the due diligence period does most of the work that inspection, financing and appraisal contingencies do in other states.
  2. After due diligence ends, you can usually back out only by risking your earnest money. Get your loan and appraisal done inside the period.
  3. A shorter due diligence period or a larger fee can make your offer stand out without raising the price.
  4. Older Kings Mountain homes, and properties with septic, wells or acreage, need time for specialist inspections.
  5. Appraisal and home-sale contingencies are added by addendum and can weaken your offer. Use them on purpose.

If you've bought a home in another state, you may expect to write an offer "contingent on inspection, financing and appraisal." North Carolina works differently. The standard Offer to Purchase and Contract used in Kings Mountain and across the state protects buyers mainly through a due diligence period. Once you understand that, deciding what to keep and what to give up gets much clearer.

How do buyer protections work in a North Carolina contract?

When you make an offer, you propose three things alongside the price:

  • A due diligence fee, paid directly to the seller and usually non-refundable, which is credited to you at closing.
  • A due diligence period, during which you can inspect, get insurance quotes, order the appraisal, finalize your loan and terminate for any reason or no reason.
  • Earnest money, held in trust. It's generally refundable if you terminate during due diligence and at risk if you fail to close afterward without a contractual reason.

The NC Real Estate Commission publishes consumer guides explaining these terms. The key point is that your protection mostly ends when due diligence ends.

How does that compare with "contingencies" elsewhere?

Protection in other statesHow it usually works in NC
Inspection contingencyInspect during the due diligence period. Negotiate repairs or terminate before it ends.
Financing contingencyThe standard form doesn't include one. Get your loan approved and the appraisal in before due diligence ends.
Appraisal contingencyOptional, by addendum. Otherwise, handle the appraisal during due diligence.
Home-sale contingencyOptional, by a contingent sale addendum.
Government-loan protectionsFHA and VA loans carry their own required appraisal language, added by addendum.

What should you never give up?

Enough time to inspect

Kings Mountain has homes from many eras: older mill-era and mid-century houses near downtown, newer subdivisions and rural properties toward Crowders Mountain and into Cleveland County. Common things to check:

  • Crawl space moisture, vapor barriers and wood-destroying insects
  • Roof age and storm wear
  • Older electrical panels and plumbing materials
  • Drainage on sloped lots
  • Septic systems and private wells outside city utility areas
  • Survey and boundary questions on acreage

Specialists such as septic, structural or well-water inspectors can take days to schedule. Shortening due diligence so much that you can't finish them is the one cut you shouldn't make.

Time for your loan to reach approval

Ask your lender how long they realistically need to get your loan through underwriting and the appraisal back. Set your due diligence period to match. For a home you're financing, this matters more than almost anything else in the offer. See mortgage pre-approval for getting ahead of it.

What can you safely trade to strengthen an offer?

  • A shorter due diligence period, if your inspector and lender can work quickly and the home is newer or recently updated.
  • A larger due diligence fee, which shows commitment. Only offer what you can afford to lose if you walk away.
  • A pre-offer inspection on a home you're serious about. It lets you shorten due diligence with confidence.
  • Appraisal flexibility, such as agreeing to cover a defined gap if you have the cash. See how to handle an appraisal gap.
  • Dropping the home-sale contingency, by selling or going under contract on your current home first.

When should you add an appraisal or home-sale contingency?

Add an appraisal contingency when you don't have cash to cover a gap and can't get the appraisal back inside due diligence, or when the home is unusual and has few comparable sales, which is common with acreage or custom homes. Add a contingent sale addendum when you genuinely can't carry two homes at once. Both protect you and both make your offer look less certain to sellers, so talk through the trade-off with your agent.

Tip: Ask for the appraisal to be ordered as soon as your contract is signed. Most low-appraisal problems happen because the appraisal came back after due diligence ended.

What about new construction and custom builds?

Builders usually use their own purchase agreements, with different deposit, change-order and termination terms. Read them closely, or see our guide to what to look for in a builder contract. Ally also runs Bohanan Construction, which builds custom homes in Gastonia.

How do you decide?

  1. List what you need to learn about this specific house before you're committed.
  2. Ask your inspector and lender how many days that will take.
  3. Decide how much due diligence fee you can afford to lose.
  4. Decide if you need appraisal or home-sale protection, or only want it.
  5. Build the offer from those answers, then adjust for competition.

Contract terms carry legal consequences. Ask a North Carolina real estate attorney about specific legal questions. When you're ready to write an offer in Kings Mountain, Talk With a Buyer's Agent.

Frequently asked questions

Is the due diligence fee refundable?

Generally not. It goes to the seller and is credited to you at closing. You'd typically get it back only if the seller breaches the contract.

What happens if the appraisal comes in low after due diligence ends?

Without an appraisal contingency, you'd usually have to make up the gap or risk your earnest money by not closing. That's why most buyers order the appraisal early in the due diligence period.

Can I make my offer depend on selling my current house?

Yes, through a contingent sale addendum. Many sellers see it as a weaker offer, so it works best when your home is already under contract or the market favors buyers.

Are contingencies different on new construction?

Builders usually use their own contracts, which have different deposit and termination terms. Have your agent review the builder's contract before you sign.
