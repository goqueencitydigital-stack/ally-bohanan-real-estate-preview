NC First-Time Home Buyer Programs: Gaston County Guide (2026)

By Ally BohananPublished May 24, 2026Updated November 16, 20267 min read

The short answer

Buying your first home in Gaston County can feel like a big leap. Between down payment, closing costs, and the day-to-day reality of moving, the numbers add up fast.

Buying your first home in Gaston County can feel like a big leap. Between down payment, closing costs, and the day-to-day reality of moving, the numbers add up fast. The good news is that North Carolina has several legitimate first-time buyer options that can make the upfront costs more manageable if you qualify.

This guide breaks down the most common NC first-time home buyer programs and how they apply to buyers shopping in Gastonia, Belmont, Mount Holly, Kings Mountain, Dallas, Lowell, and the rest of Gaston County. I will keep this practical and local: what these programs do, what lenders typically look for, and how to avoid the most common mistakes I see when buyers try to use assistance.

If you want a plan tailored to your budget and timeline, start here: contact Ally Bohanan Real Estate. We will connect you with a reputable local lender and map out the steps before you fall in love with a home that does not fit the program rules.

What counts as a “first-time home buyer” in North Carolina?

Most programs use a simple definition: you have not owned a primary residence in the last three years. Some programs also treat eligible military veterans as first-time buyers even if they have owned before. The details depend on the specific loan and assistance you are using, so your lender will confirm the definition that applies to your file.

From a real-world perspective, I tell buyers to think about “first-time” as a paperwork category, not a life stage. You can be buying your first home at 25, or buying again after renting for several years. Either way, the goal is the same: pair the right mortgage with the right amount of cash to close.

The two big buckets: mortgage options vs. down payment assistance

First-time buyer help usually shows up in one of two ways:

  • The mortgage itself may allow a low down payment, flexible funding sources, or reduced mortgage insurance for qualified buyers.
  • Down payment assistance (DPA) can provide additional funds toward your down payment and sometimes closing costs. DPA is often structured as a second lien that is deferred and may be forgiven over time, but it can also be a repayable loan or a grant depending on the program.

In Gaston County, many buyers do best when they treat these as a matching exercise. Your first step is not touring homes. Your first step is a solid pre-approval with a lender who can explain which programs you qualify for and what your cash-to-close range will be.

NC Home Advantage Mortgage and down payment assistance

One of the most talked-about state options is the NC Home Advantage Mortgage, offered through participating lenders. This is not a “one-size” product, but a set of guidelines that can pair a fixed-rate mortgage with down payment assistance for eligible borrowers.

For many first-time and move-up buyers, NC Home Advantage can include down payment assistance up to a percentage of the loan amount. For first-time buyers and eligible military veterans who qualify for NC Home Advantage, there is also a specific option called the NC 1st Home Advantage Down Payment that provides $15,000 in down payment assistance for eligible borrowers.

According to the North Carolina Housing Finance Agency, the NC 1st Home Advantage Down Payment is structured as a 0% deferred second mortgage and is forgiven 20% per year at the end of years 11 to 15, with complete forgiveness at year 15, as long as you meet the program terms (NC Housing Finance Agency).

What this means for a Gaston County buyer

If you qualify, assistance like this can reduce the amount you need to bring to closing. It can also help you keep an emergency fund, which matters more than most first-time buyers realize. In a real home, something always needs attention, and your budget should be able to handle it.

Here is the part buyers often miss: because many assistance programs are tied to specific loan rules, you need to know the limits before you shop. That includes income limits, purchase price limits (if applicable), and required education courses. Your lender will pull the official caps for the property location and household size.

Common pitfalls with down payment assistance

  • Changing jobs mid-process. A job change can be fine, but it can also add underwriting steps and delay closing.
  • Large undocumented deposits. If a family member is helping, talk to your lender first so the gift can be documented correctly.
  • Underestimating cash to close. Assistance can help, but inspections, appraisal gaps, and prepaid items can still require funds.
  • Waiting too long to pick a lender. Not every lender works with every program, and some need extra time for approvals.

Other ways first-time buyers buy in Gaston County

Not every first-time buyer uses a state program, and that is fine. A strong plan is about the right fit, not about forcing assistance into the deal. Here are common paths I see in Gastonia and surrounding towns.

Low down payment conventional loans (3% down)

Many conventional programs allow qualified borrowers to buy with as little as 3% down. These can be a great option if your credit and income fit the guidelines and you want the flexibility of a conventional loan, including the potential to remove mortgage insurance later when you reach enough equity.

Some low down payment conventional products are designed specifically for lower to moderate income buyers, and they may allow flexible sources of funds. For example, Fannie Mae notes that its HomeReady product offers down payments as low as 3% and allows a range of funding sources including gifts and grants for eligible borrowers (Fannie Mae).

FHA loans (often 3.5% down)

FHA can be helpful for buyers with limited down payment savings or buyers who need a little flexibility in credit history. The tradeoff is that FHA mortgage insurance works differently than conventional mortgage insurance. Your lender can run side-by-side numbers so you see the monthly payment difference and the long-term cost.

VA loans (0% down for eligible veterans)

If you are eligible for VA financing, this can be one of the most powerful paths to homeownership because it can allow 0% down. In a competitive market, a strong pre-approval and clean offer terms matter. I also recommend you budget for inspections and possible repairs even if your down payment is not required.

USDA loans (0% down in eligible areas)

Parts of Gaston County and nearby rural areas may qualify for USDA financing, which can offer 0% down for eligible buyers and eligible properties. USDA has location and income requirements, so the property address matters. If you are open to areas outside the city center, this is worth discussing with your lender early.

How to find legitimate first-time buyer help (and avoid bad info)

First-time buyers are targeted by bad advice online. You will see social posts claiming you can get “free money” with no strings, or that every buyer qualifies for the same grant. In reality, most programs have guidelines, and the right approach is to verify every claim with a lender and an official program page.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau recommends looking up a HUD-approved housing counseling agency to find legitimate programs and resources in your area (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau). That is a smart step if you want an independent resource to help you understand budgets, credit, and loan options.

Local step-by-step: how I recommend Gaston County buyers start

If you are planning to buy in Gaston County in 2026, here is a clean process that keeps you from wasting time or losing a home because of avoidable financing surprises.

1) Set your real budget (not the online calculator number)

Your lender will tell you what you can qualify for. Your budget is what you can comfortably pay while still saving, maintaining the home, and living your life. If you want to use the site tools as a starting point, here is our mortgage calculator, but do not stop there. Ask your lender to estimate taxes, insurance, and HOA dues for the neighborhoods you are actually considering.

2) Choose a lender who can run the program scenarios

Assistance programs are not just “yes” or “no”. The lender needs to evaluate income, credit, assets, and the property type. If you are considering NC Home Advantage, ask if they are a participating lender and how long their reservation and documentation process typically takes.

If you do not have a lender yet, our preferred lender page is a good starting point. You can also reach out directly and we will connect you with someone who is responsive and familiar with our local market.

3) Learn your “cash to close” range

Cash to close is usually more than the down payment. It includes closing costs, prepaid items (like homeowners insurance), and sometimes escrow reserves. If assistance is in play, your lender will show how that affects the total. Ask for a conservative estimate so you are not surprised.

4) Pick neighborhoods that fit your lifestyle and the loan rules

Different loan types can have different appraisal and property condition standards. That does not mean you cannot buy an older home in Gastonia with a first-time buyer loan. It just means you should be strategic about inspections and repair planning.

If you are also thinking about resale value and timing, it helps to understand current buyer demand. When you are ready, we can talk through your search and strategy using our realty services approach so you know what to expect from offer to closing.

5) Make an offer with terms that protect you

As a buyer’s agent, my job is to help you compete while still protecting you with the right inspections, financing terms, and timelines. Assistance programs can add steps, so your contract deadlines should reflect reality.

If you are early in the process, it also helps to know what happens after your offer is accepted. You can get an overview of how we work with buyers on our buy a luxury home page. Even if you are not buying luxury, the process and the standards are the same: clear guidance, thorough due diligence, and strong communication.

Straight answers

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to be a first-time buyer to use down payment assistance in NC?

Not always. Some assistance options are limited to first-time buyers or eligible veterans, while others can be available to repeat buyers who meet income and program rules. Your lender will confirm which options apply to your situation.

Is the NC 1st Home Advantage $15,000 down payment help a grant?

It is not a simple grant. The NC Housing Finance Agency describes it as a 0% deferred second mortgage that is forgiven over time if you meet the program terms, with full forgiveness at year 15 (NC Housing Finance Agency).

Can I use first-time buyer programs to purchase a townhome or condo?

Often yes, but eligibility depends on the loan type, the property, and program rules. Condos may have additional approval requirements with some loan types, so it is important to review the specific community early.

How long does it take to close with down payment assistance?

Some assistance programs add documentation and timing steps, so it can take longer than a basic conventional loan. A lender who works with the program regularly can often keep the timeline smooth. The key is to start the process early and keep paperwork organized.

What should I do if I am not sure which programs I qualify for?

Start with a conversation with a local lender and your buyer’s agent. If you want independent guidance, the CFPB recommends finding a HUD-approved housing counseling agency in your area (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau).

What is the best first step to buy a home in Gaston County?

Get pre-approved with a lender who can give you a realistic cash-to-close estimate and confirm any program eligibility. Then we can narrow your search to homes that fit your budget and timeline. When you are ready, contact Ally Bohanan Real Estate and we will build a plan.
