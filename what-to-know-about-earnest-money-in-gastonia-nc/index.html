What to Know About Earnest Money in Gastonia, NC

How earnest money works in a Gastonia purchase: how it differs from the due diligence fee, who holds it, when it's due and when you could lose it.

By Ally BohananPublished October 31, 2026Updated November 16, 20263 min read

The short answer

Earnest money is a good-faith deposit a Gastonia buyer pays after an offer is accepted. It sits in escrow, often with the closing attorney, until closing. Under North Carolina's standard contract, it's generally refunded if you terminate by the end of the due diligence period, but you could lose it if you back out later without a contract-allowed reason.

Key takeaways

  1. Earnest money and the due diligence fee are separate payments with different rules.
  2. The due diligence fee goes straight to the seller and is generally non-refundable; earnest money is held in escrow.
  3. Deadlines are written into the contract; missing one can put you in breach.
  4. The end of the due diligence period is the key date for whether earnest money is at risk.
  5. Both amounts are negotiable, and together they shape how strong your offer looks.

North Carolina handles deposits differently from many states, which can surprise buyers moving to Gastonia from elsewhere. Most purchases involve two payments: a due diligence fee and earnest money. Understanding the difference protects your money and helps you write a stronger offer.

What's the difference between earnest money and the due diligence fee?

Both count toward your cash to close, but they put your money at risk at different times. Review the details with your agent and the closing attorney before you sign.

How much earnest money is typical in Gastonia?

There's no required amount. It depends on:

  • The purchase price and property type
  • How much competition there is for the home
  • How large your due diligence fee is
  • Your financing strength
  • What the seller cares about most

When several buyers want the same home, larger deposits can help an offer stand out. In slower conditions, a modest deposit may be fine. Choose an amount that feels meaningful to the seller and that you can afford to have at risk. Your agent can tell you what's been common for similar homes recently.

When is earnest money due?

The contract states the deadline and the escrow agent. Buyers usually pay soon after the contract is signed, often by wire or certified funds, and some contracts split it into an initial deposit and additional earnest money due later. Missing the deadline can give the seller the right to terminate.

  • Know the exact deadline and amount before you sign.
  • Move funds into an account you can wire from ahead of time, especially if you're relocating.
  • Confirm wiring instructions by phone with the escrow agent at a number you've verified independently.
  • Get a receipt.

Tip: Scammers send fake wiring instructions that look like they come from attorneys or agents. Never wire money based only on an email.

When can you lose your earnest money?

Under North Carolina's standard residential contract, the due diligence period is your main window to inspect, get insurance quotes, check the appraisal and confirm financing. The general rules:

  • Terminate on or before the end of the due diligence period: earnest money is generally refunded; the seller keeps the due diligence fee.
  • Seller breaches the contract: earnest money is generally refunded, and the contract may give you other remedies.
  • Buyer backs out after the due diligence period without a contract-allowed reason: the seller may be entitled to the earnest money.

Because of this, problems with financing or appraisal that come up after the due diligence period can put your earnest money at risk. Schedule inspections early, stay in touch with your lender, and don't let the period expire with open questions. If something isn't resolved, talk with your agent about extending the period or other options before the deadline.

If the parties disagree about who gets the money, the escrow agent generally holds it until they reach agreement or the dispute is resolved. The North Carolina Real Estate Commission publishes consumer guidance on buying and on how brokers must handle trust money.

How can you use earnest money to strengthen an offer?

  • Balance it with your due diligence fee; sellers look at both.
  • Pair it with a realistic, shorter due diligence period if you're organized.
  • Match the seller's preferred closing date.
  • Back it up with a strong pre-approval.

What should sellers know?

Earnest money gives you a remedy if a buyer breaches after the due diligence period, but the due diligence fee is what you keep if they walk away during it. When comparing offers, look at both amounts, the length of the due diligence period and the buyer's financing. Learn more about selling your home in Gastonia.

Frequently asked questions

Does earnest money count toward my down payment?

Yes. It's credited toward your funds due at closing, which may include your down payment and closing costs.

Who holds earnest money in a Gastonia purchase?

The escrow agent named in the contract, usually the closing attorney or a brokerage. It stays in escrow until closing or until the contract ends.

Will I get my earnest money back if the inspection finds major problems?

Generally yes, if you terminate on or before the end of your due diligence period. The due diligence fee is usually not refunded.

Is earnest money required for out-of-state buyers?

Yes, the same contract terms apply. Plan ahead so funds can be wired on time, and verify instructions by phone.
