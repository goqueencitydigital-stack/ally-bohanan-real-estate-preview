Why Your Realtor Should Understand Construction: The Competitive Edge in Modern Real Estate

How an agent with a construction background helps you look past fresh paint, read inspection reports in context and decide which repairs are worth making.

By Ally BohananPublished April 30, 20262 min read

The short answer

A Realtor who understands construction can help you spot cosmetic cover-ups, ask the right questions about permits and major systems, read an inspection report in context, decide when to bring in a contractor or engineer, and see whether a dated house has good bones. Sellers benefit too, by focusing pre-listing work on what buyers will notice.

Key takeaways

  1. Fresh finishes can hide older systems or past problems; look underneath.
  2. Ask about permits for renovations, additions and finished basements.
  3. Inspection findings need context: some are minor, some need a specialist.
  4. Get contractor estimates before you rely on a repair budget.
  5. Sellers should prioritize repairs buyers and inspectors will notice.

Listing photos are designed to make you fall in love. New paint, staging and nice finishes can make almost any house look ready. But for most people, a home is the largest purchase they'll make, and what matters most is often behind the drywall, under the floors and in the crawl space.

That's where an agent who understands construction helps. Ally Bohanan is the business manager and designer at Bohanan Construction, a custom home builder in Gastonia, alongside her work as a broker. That background shapes how she looks at houses for buyers and sellers.

How does it help you see past a quick flip?

Many renovated homes are done well. Some aren't. A construction-minded agent looks beyond the new countertops and asks:

  • Fresh paint in one spot: a design choice, or covering a water stain?
  • New flooring: is it hiding uneven subfloor or soft spots?
  • Cabinet and door alignment: could it point to settling floors?
  • New appliances: was the electrical panel updated to support them?
  • Finished basement or addition: were permits pulled?

Those questions shape your inspection plan, and tell you when to bring in a specialist.

How does it help with renovation and repair planning?

When you're considering a home that needs work, the key questions are what the project involves and who should price it.

  • Load-bearing walls: opening a wall may need an engineer and beams, which changes the budget and timeline
  • System lifespans: roof, HVAC and water heater age help you plan the next several years
  • Scope: some projects are cosmetic; others touch structure, plumbing or electrical and need permits

Get written estimates from licensed contractors during due diligence before you count on a number. Our guide to custom home building in Gastonia covers more of the planning side.

How does it help during inspection?

Inspection reports can be long and full of alarming phrases. A construction-minded agent helps you separate:

  • Minor items: grading touch-ups, caulking, loose fixtures
  • Major systems: roof, HVAC, electrical, plumbing
  • Items that need a specialist: foundation cracks, moisture patterns, structural changes

That context helps you avoid walking away over something small or missing something serious. See what to look for in inspections and how to negotiate repairs.

How does it help sellers before listing?

Not every repair is worth doing before a sale. A walk-through with an eye for construction can help you:

  • Fix the items a buyer's inspector is likely to flag
  • Choose a refresh over a full remodel when it makes sense, such as a new vanity, lighting and regrouting instead of a whole new bathroom
  • Gather permits and records for past work
  • Decide what to repair and what to price for

Our top upgrades that boost value guide has more ideas.

How does it help you find homes with good bones?

Some of the best opportunities are houses that look dated but are solidly built. A construction lens helps you see past old finishes to a sound foundation, a workable layout and quality materials, and to recognize when a house's problems go deeper than cosmetics.

Does the help end at closing?

If you're planning an addition or a renovation later, it helps to have someone who understands how building decisions affect future resale. If you're weighing whether to buy or build, Ally can talk through both paths.

Straight answers

Frequently asked questions

Can my agent tell me what a repair will cost?

An agent with construction experience can help you understand the scope and which professional to call, but get written estimates from licensed contractors before you rely on a number.

Does a construction background replace a home inspection?

No. A licensed home inspector, and specialists when needed, should always evaluate the home. The agent's background helps you understand and act on what they find.

How do I check whether renovations were permitted?

Ask the seller for permits and records, and check with the local permitting office for the city or county where the home is located. Your agent can help you know what to ask for.
