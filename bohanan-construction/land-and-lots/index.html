Bohanan Construction · Land and lots

Finding land, and building on your lot.

Your land shapes your home more than any finish you choose. Here is what to look at, whether you are still searching or already own a lot.

Talk About My Land

The short answer

Before you build, confirm six things about the land: how it is accessed, which utilities reach it, whether the soil supports septic if needed, its slope and drainage, any restrictions and setbacks, and who handles permits. Bohanan Construction reviews these with you, and Ally, a licensed broker, can help you find and evaluate land first.

Starting point 02

I already own a lot.

Your land is the starting point of the design. Where the sun falls, how the lot slopes, where the road and utilities are, and what the restrictions allow all shape where the house sits and how it is laid out.

Bring what you have to the first conversation: the address or parcel number, a survey or plat, any HOA documents, and photos of the site. The team will help confirm the jurisdiction and site considerations for your build.

What the team looks at first

The site evaluation checklist

Six things to understand about any lot before the design is final. This is a general guide, not an evaluation of a particular property.

  1. 01

    Access and driveway

    How the lot meets the road, any shared driveways or easements, and how long the driveway needs to be. On larger parcels the driveway can be one of the first big decisions.

  2. 02

    Utilities

    Whether public water and sewer reach the lot, or whether the home will need a private well and septic system. Power and internet service to the site are worth confirming too.

  3. 03

    Soil, perc and septic

    If the lot needs septic, the soil has to support it. A soil evaluation (often called a perc test) through the county shows where a system can go and can shape where the house sits.

  4. 04

    Slope, clearing and drainage

    Topography, tree clearing and where water runs during a storm all affect the foundation and grading plan. Flood maps belong in this check as well.

  5. 05

    Restrictions and setbacks

    Recorded covenants, HOA or architectural guidelines, minimum home sizes, and setback lines from the survey or plat decide what can be built and where.

  6. 06

    Jurisdiction and permitting

    Whether the property falls under a city or under Gaston County for permitting. Kings Mountain, for example, spans both Gaston and Cleveland counties, so the exact parcel matters.

Acreage and rural homesites

More land, bigger decisions.

Many Bohanan clients build on acreage, from a five-acre homesite to a property of more than thirty acres. Larger land brings bigger decisions about siting, views, the driveway and private utilities, and the team helps you weigh them.

Straight answers

Land and lot questions

What should I check before buying land to build on?

Start with access, utilities, soil (if the lot needs septic), slope and drainage, recorded restrictions and setbacks, and which jurisdiction handles permits. Ally can help you look at a property before you make an offer, and Bohanan Construction can talk through what it would mean for a build.

What is a perc test?

It is the common name for a soil evaluation that shows whether, and where, the ground can support a septic system. If a lot cannot connect to public sewer, the results can decide where the house and the septic field go.

Can I build on a lot I already own?

Yes. Many Bohanan clients come to the team already owning land, from neighborhood lots to acreage. The first step is a conversation about the property and what it needs.

Can Ally help me find land?

Yes. Ally Bohanan is a licensed real estate broker with Ally Bohanan Real Estate. She can help you search for and evaluate land, and coordinate with Bohanan Construction so the lot and the home plan fit together. See buying with Ally.

Does a big lot change the build?

Usually. Larger land brings bigger decisions about siting, views, driveway length, clearing and private utilities. Bohanan has built on acreage, from a five-acre homesite to a property of more than thirty acres.
