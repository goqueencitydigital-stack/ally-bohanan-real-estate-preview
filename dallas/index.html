Day to day

Dallas day to day: roads and the square

Getting around

The Town of Dallas calls itself the Crossroads of Gaston County, and it sits just north of the I-85 and US 321 interchange. US 321 runs north past Gaston College toward Lincolnton; from I-85, exit 17 is the college’s posted route onto US 321 North. NC 279, the Dallas-Cherryville Highway, heads west to Cherryville, and Gastonia is directly south.

Schools and services

Dallas is in Gaston County Schools. Assignment follows the home address, so run the exact house through the district’s school search rather than going by the town name. For college classes and workforce training, Gaston College keeps its main campus at 201 Highway US 321 South, inside Dallas.

Landmarks and weekends

Dallas was named for Vice President George M. Dallas and served as the first county seat. The 1848 courthouse on the square was restored in 2015, and the Gaston County Museum of Art & History fills the 1852 Hoffman Hotel, including what is described as the state’s largest collection of horse-drawn vehicles. The Historic Town Square at 131 N. Gaston St. is where downtown events gather.

Straight answers

Dallas questions

Was Dallas the Gaston County seat?

Yes. Dallas was the first county seat, which is why it has a courthouse square. The seat later moved to Gastonia, and the 1848 courthouse in Dallas was restored in 2015.

Is Gaston College in Dallas?

Yes. Gaston College lists its main campus at 201 Highway US 321 South in Dallas, reached from I-85 at exit 17.

Is every Dallas address inside the town limits?

No. Some Dallas mailing addresses are in unincorporated Gaston County. Town limits change which services you get and whether a town tax applies, so confirm it on the county’s parcel records before you compare homes.

Which schools serve Dallas?

Gaston County Schools, assigned by address. Look up the specific home with the district’s school address search.

What is at the Gaston County Museum of Art & History?

The museum fills the 1852 Hoffman Hotel on the Dallas square. It is known for what is described as the largest collection of horse-drawn vehicles in North Carolina, alongside its art and county history exhibits.
