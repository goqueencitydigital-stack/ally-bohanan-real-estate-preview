How to Make Your Build Timeline Realistic in Dallas, NC

The phases of a custom home build in Dallas, NC, what usually causes delays, and how to plan your financing and move around them.

By Ally BohananPublished July 7, 2026Updated November 16, 20262 min read

In this article

  2. What should you check before buying land?

  6. Frequently asked questions

The short answer

A realistic custom build timeline in Dallas, NC covers pre-construction work like land due diligence, design, septic or sewer approval and permits, then construction, inspections and final punch list. Ask your builder for a written schedule, finish your selections early, build in a cushion for weather and inspections, and align rate locks and housing plans with that cushion.

Key takeaways

  1. Pre-construction work, including land checks, design and permits, often takes longer than people expect.
  2. Confirm zoning, utilities and septic feasibility before you buy the lot.
  3. Make selections early; late changes are a leading cause of delay.
  4. Weather, inspections and material lead times need a cushion in the schedule.
  5. Line up your construction loan, rate lock and temporary housing with the realistic end date, not the best case.

Dallas, just north of Gastonia, offers in-town lots near its historic downtown as well as larger rural parcels nearby. That variety affects your timeline: a lot with town water and sewer moves differently than acreage that needs a well, septic permit and a long driveway. Planning around those differences from the start keeps a build from feeling rushed or stalled.

What should you check before buying land?

  • Jurisdiction: is the lot inside the Town of Dallas or in unincorporated Gaston County? Zoning and permitting differ.
  • Utilities: town water and sewer, or well and septic? Septic permits go through Gaston County and depend on soil evaluation.
  • Flood zone: check the NC Flood Risk Information System.
  • Access: road frontage, driveway permit requirements and easements.
  • Restrictions: HOA or deed covenants on home size, materials or outbuildings.

Our land and lots page and guide to buying land for a custom home go deeper.

How do you keep design and selections from slowing things down?

  1. Set your budget before final plans so you don't redesign later.
  2. Ask your builder for a selection schedule with deadlines.
  3. Choose long-lead items such as windows, cabinets and specialty fixtures first.
  4. Limit change orders after construction starts. Each one can affect cost and schedule.
  5. Keep a single decision-maker or a clear process if more than one person is choosing.

How should you plan financing and housing?

  • Ask your lender how draws work and how long the construction loan term is.
  • Understand rate lock options and what happens if the build runs long.
  • If you're selling your current home, plan for a possible gap with a rent-back, short-term rental or flexible closing date.
  • Set your move date from the builder's realistic schedule plus a cushion.

What questions should you ask your builder?

  • Can I see a written schedule broken down by phase?
  • How often will we get updates, and in what form?
  • How are weather days and change orders handled in the contract?
  • Who is my day-to-day contact?
  • Is your general contractor license current? You can check with the NC Licensing Board for General Contractors.

Straight answers

Frequently asked questions

Can I start building right after I close on land?

Usually not. Plans, engineering, financing and permits typically come first, and some of that work can start during your land due diligence period.

What's the most common cause of build delays?

It varies, but weather, permit or inspection timing, material lead times and late owner changes come up often. Clear selections and a schedule cushion help.

Should I sell my current home before building?

It depends on your finances and tolerance for moving twice. Ask your lender how each option affects your construction loan, and plan housing for the gap.
