How should you prepare for a first conversation?

Bring the facts you already know and the questions you do not want overlooked. Buyers can outline timing, financing, property type and deal breakers. Sellers can share the address, timing, known condition concerns and any documents. A useful first conversation separates confirmed facts from assumptions and identifies which details need a lender, inspector, attorney, appraiser or insurer.

Choosing well

How do you choose the right Realtor in Gastonia?

  • Local knowledge. Ask which Gastonia neighborhoods they have sold in recently, and check the record.
  • How they price. A price should come from a walk-through and recent sales, explained in plain English.
  • How they communicate. How often you will hear from them, and how they track deadlines.
  • What they know about houses. Condition and construction matter as much as finishes.
  • Their license. Confirm the person’s current license and firm on the NC Real Estate Commission site before sharing sensitive information.

Local knowledge

What should you know about Gastonia?

Gastonia is Gaston County’s largest city and its county seat, just west of Charlotte along I-85. Its textile history still shapes the city: three neighborhoods are National Register historic districts, Loray Mill, Downtown and York-Chester, and York-Chester and Brookwood are also local historic districts, where exterior changes go through design review.

Downtown, the Franklin Urban Sports and Entertainment District, known as FUSE, is anchored by CaroMont Health Park, which opened in 2021 and is home to the Gastonia Ghost Peppers. Beyond the center, Gastonia ranges from mill-village bungalows to newer subdivisions and land at the edges of the city. The Gastonia guide covers it in depth.

Questions about choosing a Realtor

How do I check a Realtor’s license in North Carolina?

Use the license lookup on the NC Real Estate Commission website. Ally Margaret Bohanan holds North Carolina broker license 315170 and is associated with Northstar Real Estate, LLC, firm license C29596.

What is the difference between a Realtor and a real estate broker?

In North Carolina, anyone licensed to sell real estate for others is a broker licensed by the Real Estate Commission. Realtor is a trademark for members of the National Association of Realtors, who agree to its code of ethics.

Do I have to sign an agreement to talk with a Realtor?

No. A first conversation is free. You will review the state’s Working With Real Estate Agents disclosure at the first substantial meeting, and a written agreement is required before a buyer’s agent makes an offer or a listing agent markets your home.
