How to Prep Your Home for Professional Photos in Belmont, NC

A room-by-room, day-before and day-of checklist so your Belmont listing photos look bright, spacious and ready for the first buyer who scrolls past.

The short answer

Before listing photos, finish repairs, then deep clean and declutter every surface the camera will see. Stage each room for one clear purpose, open every window treatment and use one bulb color throughout. Hide cords, bins and personal items. On the day, clear the driveway, turn on every light and stay out of the frame.

Key takeaways

  1. Finish repairs, painting and landscaping before the photo appointment, not after.
  2. Clear countertops, fridge doors, bathroom vanities and the floors in every room.
  3. Use one bulb color temperature throughout and replace every burned-out bulb.
  4. Take cars, trash bins, hoses and pet items out of every exterior shot.
  5. Ask your photographer about twilight and aerial shots for homes near the water or on larger lots.

Almost every buyer meets your home online before they ever see it in person. In Belmont, where buyers often compare listings in town with homes in Mount Holly, Gastonia and west Charlotte, your photos have to earn the showing. Here's how to prepare so the photographer can capture your home at its best.

What needs to be finished before photo day?

  • All repairs and touch-up painting
  • Landscaping: mowed, edged, mulched and trimmed back from windows
  • Pressure washing the driveway, walkway and siding
  • Decluttering and packing out extra furniture (see our decluttering plan)
  • Any staging pieces delivered and placed

Photos last as long as the listing does. Don't book the shoot until the house is ready.

How should each room look?

RoomPrep checklist
KitchenBare counters except one or two styled items. Nothing on the fridge. Towels, sponges, soap and trash can put away.
Living areasFewer furniture pieces, pillows straightened, remotes and cords hidden, rugs squared to the room
BedroomsBeds made with light, neutral bedding. Nightstands cleared. Nothing under the beds that can be seen.
BathroomsVanities cleared, toilet lids down, fresh matching towels, shower bottles removed, mirrors polished
Office or flex roomOne clear purpose. Papers and cables out of sight.
Laundry and garageSupplies put away, floor swept, garage doors closed unless the garage is being shot
ClosetsMatching hangers and some empty space (see closet tips)

How do you get the most light?

  1. Clean the windows inside and out, and remove screens if you can.
  2. Open every blind, shade and curtain fully.
  3. Replace every bulb with the same color temperature.
  4. Turn on every light, including lamps, under-cabinet lights and exterior fixtures.
  5. Turn off ceiling fans and TVs.

Our guide to boosting natural light has more ideas for dim rooms.

What about the exterior shots?

The front exterior is usually the lead photo, and it decides whether many buyers click on your listing.

  • Move all cars off the driveway and away from the curb in front of the house.
  • Put trash and recycling bins, hoses, toys and tools out of sight.
  • Clean the front door and hardware. Add one simple planter or wreath.
  • Stage the porch and patio with clean seating. Remove grill covers and cushions that look worn.
  • Turn on porch and landscape lights if you're getting twilight shots.

Are twilight and drone shots worth it?

A twilight exterior, with the lights glowing inside, can make a listing stand out in search results. Aerial photos help buyers understand a larger lot, a home near the river or how close a house is to downtown Belmont. Commercial drone pilots have to follow FAA rules, and flights close to Charlotte Douglas International Airport may need airspace authorization, so use a licensed operator.

What should you do the day before?

  • Do a final wipe-down of counters, mirrors, glass, stainless steel and floors.
  • Vacuum, leaving fresh lines in the carpet.
  • Box up anything still out into one closet the photographer won't shoot.
  • Charge any smart lights and set timers for exterior lights.

What should you do on the day?

  1. Open the window treatments and turn on all the lights before the photographer arrives.
  2. Hide pet bowls, beds and litter boxes, and take the pets out.
  3. Put away toiletries, chargers and personal paperwork.
  4. Leave, or stay in one room and keep out of the shot.
  5. Ask the photographer for a quick preview of the main exterior and kitchen images.

Tip: Before the photographer arrives, take a quick phone photo of each room from its doorway. Clutter you've stopped noticing jumps out on a small screen.

Should you stage first?

If rooms are empty or furniture is oversized or worn, staging even a few key rooms helps buyers understand scale. Our home staging page explains how it works.

Frequently asked questions

How far ahead should I schedule the photographer?

Book once your prep work is nearly done, and leave a day or two of cushion. Rescheduling for a sunnier day is better than shooting a gray exterior.

Should I be home during the shoot?

It's usually easier if you aren't. If you stay, keep to one room, and the photographer will shoot that room last. Take pets out of the house or keep them crated in the car.

Are drone photos worth it?

Often, for homes near the river, on larger lots or close to downtown amenities. Commercial drone pilots must follow FAA rules, and airspace near Charlotte Douglas may need authorization, so hire a licensed operator.

Should I remove family photos?

Yes, for the photos at least. Buyers should picture themselves in the home, and removing personal items also protects your privacy online.
