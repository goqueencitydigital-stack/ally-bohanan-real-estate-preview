Best Questions to Ask Your Lender in Lincolnton, NC

A question-by-question script for interviewing mortgage lenders before you buy in Lincolnton or anywhere in Lincoln County.

By Ally BohananPublished October 6, 2026Updated November 16, 20263 min read

Lincolnton

The short answer

Ask each lender which loan programs fit you, what your rate and total closing costs will be on a written Loan Estimate, how rate locks work, what documents they need, if you qualify for NC Housing Finance Agency or USDA programs, and how fast they can close. Asking two or three lenders the same questions lets you compare them fairly.

Key takeaways

  1. Interview at least two or three lenders and compare their official Loan Estimates line by line.
  2. Ask about loan programs that match the property: FHA, VA, USDA, conventional, jumbo or construction.
  3. Some parts of Lincoln County may qualify for USDA loans. Check the address on USDA's eligibility map.
  4. Find out how the lock works: how long it lasts, what it costs to extend and whether the rate can float down.
  5. Ask who your day-to-day contact is and how fast the lender typically closes.

Your lender affects your price range, your monthly payment, how strong your offer looks and if you close on time. Still, plenty of buyers in Lincolnton go with the first lender who returns their call. Use the questions below to interview two or three lenders, and put the answers side by side.

Which loan programs fit me and this kind of property?

Lincolnton has in-town homes near downtown, older houses, newer subdivisions and rural acreage across Lincoln County. Each can point to a different loan. Ask:

  • Do I fit best with conventional, FHA, VA or USDA financing, and why?
  • If my price is near the conforming loan limit, when would I need a jumbo loan?
  • Is this address eligible for a USDA loan? (You can check it yourself on the USDA eligibility map.)
  • If the home needs work, do you offer renovation loans such as FHA 203(k)?
  • If I want to buy land and build, do you offer construction or construction-to-permanent loans?
  • How might my loan type affect how a seller views my offer?

If building is on your mind, Ally also runs Bohanan Construction, which builds custom homes in Gastonia, and our buy or build guide compares the two paths.

What will my rate and costs really be?

A rate quoted over the phone isn't a quote. Ask for an official Loan Estimate. Every lender uses the same federal form, so you can compare them line by line.

  • What rate and APR are you quoting, and does that include discount points?
  • What are your origination and underwriting fees?
  • How would my payment change if the rate moved up or down by a small amount?
  • Would a seller-paid or builder-paid buydown help me, and is it temporary or permanent?
  • Are any lender credits available, and what do they cost me in rate?

You can test scenarios with our mortgage calculator before you call.

How does your rate lock work?

  • When can I lock, and for how many days?
  • What does it cost to extend the lock if closing slips?
  • Do you offer a float-down if rates fall after I lock?

What will you review, and what should I avoid doing?

Ask for the full document list up front, especially if you're self-employed, earn commission or bonus income, or are moving from another state.

  • How do you calculate self-employed, commission or variable income?
  • Which accounts can I use for the down payment and reserves?
  • How do I document a gift from family?
  • What credit score and debt-to-income range does my loan program require?
  • What should I avoid between now and closing, such as new credit, large deposits or job changes?

Do I qualify for any assistance programs?

Many buyers never ask about these programs. Ask the lender whether they're approved to offer the NC Housing Finance Agency home-buyer programs, and if you meet the income and purchase-price limits. Also ask:

  • Can assistance be combined with seller-paid closing costs?
  • Does the program require a homebuyer education course, and how long does it take?
  • Does using assistance change my rate or the closing timeline?

First-time buyers should also read what first-time buyers in Gaston County need to know.

How will closing costs and timing be handled?

In North Carolina, closings are handled by a real estate attorney, and your lender has to send you a Closing Disclosure at least three business days before you close. Ask:

  • How long do your approvals and appraisals typically take for homes in Lincoln County right now?
  • What usually delays your closings, and how do you prevent it?
  • Will you work directly with my agent and the closing attorney?
  • When will I receive my final numbers?

Why are you the right lender for me?

  • How often do you close loans in Lincolnton and the surrounding area?
  • Who will be my day-to-day contact, and how quickly do you respond?
  • Can you call the listing agent to confirm my approval when I make an offer?
  • Do you service my loan after closing, or sell it?

Tip: Ask every lender the same questions on the same day, and request the Loan Estimates for the same loan amount. That's the only way to compare them fairly.

Straight answers

Frequently asked questions

How early should I talk to a lender?

Before you tour homes. A full pre-approval tells you your real price range and makes your offer more credible to Lincolnton sellers.

Does shopping several lenders hurt my credit?

Credit scoring models generally treat several mortgage inquiries within a short window as a single inquiry. Ask each lender whether they need a hard pull at the pre-approval stage.

Is a lender's Loan Estimate binding?

Some fees can't increase at all and others can rise only within limits, under federal rules. The CFPB's Loan Estimate guide explains which fees fall into which category.

Can I switch lenders after I'm under contract?

You can, but it risks delays against your contract's closing date. If you plan to compare lenders, do it before you write an offer.
