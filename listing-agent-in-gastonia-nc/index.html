Listing representation

What does a listing agent do for you?

  • Pricing. An in-person pricing consultation grounded in recent sales and today’s competition.
  • Preparation. A builder’s view of which repairs pay for themselves, plus staging guidance for the rooms that matter.
  • Presentation. Photography and a listing description that tell buyers what the photos cannot.
  • Showings. A schedule that fits your life, with feedback after each tour.
  • Negotiation. Price is one term. Ally weighs the due diligence fee, earnest money, financing strength, closing date and repair requests.
  • Closing. Disclosures, deadlines, appraisal and repair negotiations, coordinated with the buyer’s agent and the closing attorney.

Selling, step by step

What are the steps to sell a home in North Carolina?

Six stages, from the first walk-through to the closing table.

  1. Pricing consultation

    Ally walks through the home and explains the range she would list in and why.

  2. Listing agreement and disclosures

    You sign a written listing agreement, review the Working With Real Estate Agents disclosure and complete the state’s property and mineral rights disclosure forms.

  3. Prepare the home

    Repairs, decluttering, staging and a deep clean, in the order the selling timeline lays out.

  4. Launch

    Photography, the listing description and the first weekend of showings.

  5. Review offers

    Ally compares every term, not just price, and negotiates on your behalf.

  6. Due diligence and closing

    The buyer inspects and appraises. Ally handles repair requests and keeps every deadline in view until the closing attorney records the deed.

Straight answers

Seller questions

Do I need a written listing agreement?

Yes. North Carolina requires a written listing agreement before a broker markets your property. It sets the list price, the length of the agreement, the broker’s duties and the compensation. Ally reviews every term with you before you sign.

What disclosures does a North Carolina seller have to give?

Most residential sellers must give buyers the Residential Property and Owners’ Association Disclosure Statement and the Mineral and Oil and Gas Rights Mandatory Disclosure Statement, no later than when the buyer makes an offer. Answering “no representation” is allowed, but Ally must still disclose material facts she knows about.

What closing costs does a seller pay in North Carolina?

Common seller costs include the excise tax on the deed, which is $1 for every $500 of the sale price, plus the mortgage payoff, any agreed repairs or credits, prorated property taxes and the listing compensation in your agreement. Your closing attorney will give you the final figures.

What is the due diligence fee, and who keeps it?

The buyer pays the due diligence fee directly to you, the seller, for the right to inspect and to terminate for any reason during the due diligence period. If the buyer walks away in that window, you generally keep the fee. If the sale closes, it is credited to the buyer.

How quickly will my home sell?

That depends on price, condition, location and the season. Amy Gordon’s review describes going under contract in under six days on one home and under ten on another, and Angel Harris describes multiple offers within days. Those are real results, not a promise. Ally will tell you what she sees in your market.
