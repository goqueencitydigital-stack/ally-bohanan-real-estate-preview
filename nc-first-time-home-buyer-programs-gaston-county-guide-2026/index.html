NC First-Time Home Buyer Programs: Gaston County Guide (2026)

A program-by-program look at NC Housing Finance Agency assistance and low-down-payment loans for first-time buyers in Gaston County.

The short answer

First-time buyers in Gaston County can combine a low-down-payment loan (conventional, FHA, VA or USDA) with North Carolina Housing Finance Agency help, such as the NC Home Advantage Mortgage's down payment assistance or the $15,000 NC 1st Home Advantage Down Payment. Eligibility depends on income, price limits, credit and a participating lender, so confirm details with NCHFA and your lender.

Key takeaways

  1. Most programs define a first-time buyer as someone who hasn't owned a principal residence in the past three years.
  2. Help comes from two places: the loan itself and separate down payment assistance.
  3. NCHFA's NC 1st Home Advantage Down Payment is a 0% deferred second mortgage, not a simple grant.
  4. You need a participating lender, and income and sales price limits apply.
  5. Assistance reduces cash to close but doesn't cover everything; budget for inspections and reserves.

Down payment and closing costs are the biggest hurdle for most first-time buyers in Gaston County. North Carolina has legitimate programs that can help if you qualify. This guide walks through each main option, how it works and how to use it when buying in Gastonia, Belmont, Mount Holly, Kings Mountain, Dallas, Lowell or elsewhere in the county.

Who counts as a first-time home buyer?

Most programs use the same definition: you haven't owned a home as your principal residence in the past three years. That means you may qualify again even if you've owned before. Some programs also include military veterans, or buyers purchasing in certain targeted areas, regardless of past ownership. Your lender confirms which definition applies to you.

How is first-time buyer help structured?

  • The loan itself: some mortgages allow a low or zero down payment, flexible sources of funds, or reduced mortgage insurance.
  • Down payment assistance (DPA): extra funds toward your down payment, often set up as a deferred or forgivable second mortgage.

The two often work together. The right combination depends on your income, credit, savings and the home you buy.

What does the NC Home Advantage Mortgage offer?

The North Carolina Housing Finance Agency (NCHFA) offers the NC Home Advantage Mortgage through participating lenders. According to NCHFA, it offers down payment assistance of up to 3% of the loan amount and is available to both first-time and move-up buyers who meet program requirements. It pairs a fixed-rate mortgage with that assistance.

What is the NC 1st Home Advantage Down Payment?

For buyers who qualify for the NC Home Advantage Mortgage, NCHFA also offers the NC 1st Home Advantage Down Payment. According to NCHFA, it provides:

  • $15,000 in down payment assistance
  • As a 0% deferred second mortgage
  • Forgiven 20% per year at the end of years 11 through 15, and fully forgiven at the end of year 15

NCHFA lists eligibility requirements including being a first-time buyer, a military veteran or buying in a targeted census tract; meeting income and sales price limits; living in the home as your principal residence; and meeting a minimum credit score. Check the program page for the current requirements before you plan around it.

Because it's a second mortgage, selling or refinancing before it's forgiven can mean repaying it. Ask your lender how that works for your situation.

Parts of Gaston County outside city centers may be USDA-eligible, so it's worth checking if you're open to more rural areas.

What mistakes trip up buyers using assistance?

  • Choosing a lender too late. Not every lender offers every program, and some need extra time.
  • Shopping above the limits. Know your income and sales price caps before you tour.
  • Undocumented deposits. If family is helping, ask your lender how to document a gift first.
  • Underestimating cash to close. Inspections, due diligence fee, earnest money, prepaid items and any appraisal gap still need funds.
  • Changing jobs or adding debt during the process.
  • Short contract deadlines. Assistance can add steps, so set realistic timelines in your offer.

How do you avoid bad information?

Be wary of social posts promising "free money" with no strings. Verify every program on its official page and with a participating lender. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau suggests working with a HUD-approved housing counseling agency for independent help.

What's the best way to start in Gaston County?

  1. Set your real budget: what you can comfortably pay while still saving. Our mortgage calculator is a starting point.
  2. Talk with a participating lender who can compare program scenarios side by side. See our preferred lender page.
  3. Get a conservative cash-to-close estimate, including closing costs, prepaid items and reserves.
  4. Pick areas that fit your budget and the program rules, and plan inspections carefully on older homes, since some loans have property condition standards.
  5. Make an offer with realistic deadlines that protect you.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to be a first-time buyer to get down payment help in NC?

Not always. According to NCHFA, the NC Home Advantage Mortgage's assistance is available to move-up buyers too, while the NC 1st Home Advantage Down Payment is limited to first-time buyers, veterans and buyers in targeted areas.

Is the $15,000 NC 1st Home Advantage Down Payment a grant?

No. NCHFA describes it as a 0% deferred second mortgage that is forgiven over years 11 through 15 if you meet the program terms.

Can I use these programs for a townhome or condo?

Often yes, but it depends on the loan type and property. Condos may need additional approval, so review the community with your lender early.

Does down payment assistance slow down closing?

It can add documentation steps. A lender who uses the program regularly can usually keep things on schedule if you start early and stay organized.
