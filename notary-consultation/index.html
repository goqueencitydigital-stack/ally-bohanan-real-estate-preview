Notary, step by step

How does a notary appointment work?

  1. Get in touch

    Use the form below or call the office. Tell Ally what kind of document it is and how many people are signing.

  2. Bring ID and the unsigned document

    Each signer needs a current government-issued photo ID. Do not sign before the appointment.

  3. Sign in front of the notary

    The notary confirms each signer’s identity, watches the signature and completes the certificate.

  4. Take it where it needs to go

    Your lender, attorney, county office or the person who asked for it.

Straight answers

Notary and consultation questions

What should I bring to a notary appointment?

A current government-issued photo ID for every person signing, and the complete document, unsigned. Every signer needs to be present. If you are unsure whether your document needs a notary, ask whoever requested it.

How much does a notary charge in North Carolina?

North Carolina law sets maximum fees. For acknowledgments, jurats, verifications and proofs, a notary may charge up to $10 per principal signature, with higher limits for electronic and remote notarizations. Ally will confirm any fee before your appointment.

Can a notary give legal advice?

No. A notary who is not an attorney cannot give legal advice, explain the legal effect of a document or choose which form you need. For those questions, talk with a North Carolina attorney.

What is a real estate consultation?

A one-on-one conversation for people who are not ready to buy or sell yet. Ally can help you understand your home’s market position, weigh selling against holding, plan a purchase or think through land and investment questions.

Can you notarize closing documents for a home sale?

Ally’s notary services cover real estate documents such as contracts, affidavits and closing paperwork. Your closing attorney or lender may have specific signing instructions, so share them when you book.

How do I check that a notary is commissioned?

The North Carolina Secretary of State has a public notary search where you can look up any commissioned notary.

Sources checked November 2026: NC G.S. 10B-31: notary fees · NC Secretary of State notary search. Rules, rates and forms change, so confirm the current version with each source or with your attorney, lender or tax adviser.
