About this topic

How buying a home works here

The short answer

Buying a home in Gaston County follows a set path: get pre-approved, put your buyer agency agreement in writing, tour, make an offer on North Carolina’s standard Offer to Purchase and Contract, use the due diligence period to inspect and appraise, then close. The guides below explain each step for Gastonia, Belmont, Mount Holly and nearby towns, and for York County, SC across Lake Wylie.

Buying here means choosing between very different places close together: older mill-village streets in McAdenville and Cramerton, newer neighborhoods around Belmont and Mount Holly near the Catawba River, more land toward Dallas, Cherryville and Kings Mountain, and the South Carolina side of Lake Wylie in York County. Rules and costs change at the state line, so know which side you are shopping on before you start.

In North Carolina most resale homes are bought on the standard Offer to Purchase and Contract (Form 2‑T). It gives the buyer a negotiated due diligence period to arrange inspections, an appraisal, a survey and insurance quotes, in exchange for a due diligence fee paid to the seller. Earnest money is a separate deposit, usually held in a trust or escrow account. The North Carolina Real Estate Commission explains both in its consumer guides.

On the lending side, federal rules give you two documents to watch: a Loan Estimate within three business days of applying, and a Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Comparing Loan Estimates side by side shows the real difference between lenders. Our preferred lender page and mortgage calculator help you get started.

Straight answers

Questions readers ask

Do I need a written agreement with a buyer’s agent in North Carolina?

Yes, before any offer. North Carolina Real Estate Commission rules require a buyer agency agreement to be in writing and signed no later than the time an offer is made, and the agreement should clearly state how your agent will be paid. You can sign it at the start so expectations are clear from the first showing.

What is the difference between earnest money and a due diligence fee?

Earnest money is a good-faith deposit made with your offer. A due diligence fee is a separate, negotiated amount paid to the seller in exchange for your right to end the contract for any reason during the due diligence period. Due diligence fees usually go straight to the seller rather than into a trust account. See our earnest money guide.

When will I see my final loan costs?

Your lender must send a Loan Estimate within three business days of receiving your application, and you must receive the Closing Disclosure, with the final terms and closing costs, at least three business days before closing. Use those days to compare the two and ask about any changes.

Is buying in South Carolina different?

In a few important ways. South Carolina sellers must give buyers the state’s Residential Property Condition Disclosure Statement before the contract is signed, and a home you live in as your primary residence can qualify for the 4% legal-residence assessment ratio for property tax if you apply with the county assessor.
