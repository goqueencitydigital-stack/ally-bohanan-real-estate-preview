Best Neighborhoods for Homebuyers in Belmont, NC

By Ally BohananPublished June 15, 202610 min read

Belmont

The short answer

Belmont, North Carolina, has quickly become one of the most desirable places to live in the entire Charlotte metro area. Nestled along the South Fork Catawba River in Gaston County, this charming city offers a rare combination of small-town character, strong community spirit, top-rated schools, and easy access to everything Charlotte has to offer. For homebuyers looking to plant roots in a place that truly feels like home, Belmont delivers at every price point.

This guide walks you through the best neighborhoods in Belmont, NC, what makes each one special, and what you can expect to pay in today’s market.

Why Belmont, NC Is a Top Destination for Homebuyers

Before diving into individual neighborhoods, it helps to understand why so many buyers are choosing Belmont in the first place.

Belmont sits just 15 minutes west of uptown Charlotte via I-85, making it a natural choice for professionals who work in the city but want a quieter, more affordable place to come home to. The city’s historic downtown is filled with locally owned restaurants, boutique shops, breweries, and community events that give it a genuinely walkable, vibrant feel.

Gaston County’s property taxes are notably lower than Mecklenburg County next door, which means buyers often get significantly more home for their money. Schools in the Gaston County system, including those serving Belmont, have strong reputations for academic programs, athletics, and community involvement.

Add in proximity to Lake Wylie, the South Fork River Greenway, and Daniel Stowe Botanical Garden, and it becomes clear why buyer demand in Belmont has been strong and consistent.

1. Downtown Belmont and the Historic District

For buyers who want to walk out their front door and be in the heart of the action, Downtown Belmont is the clear choice. This neighborhood centers around Main Street and Elm Street, where historic bungalows and Craftsman-style homes sit alongside newer infill construction.

Properties in the historic district tend to range from modest two-bedroom cottages to beautifully renovated four-bedroom homes with original character and modern finishes. Pricing typically starts in the upper $200,000s for smaller homes and climbs above $600,000 for larger, fully updated properties on prime streets.

What sets Downtown Belmont apart is the lifestyle it offers. Residents can walk to dinner at acclaimed local restaurants, catch live music at one of the craft breweries, shop at the farmers market, and participate in a calendar full of festivals and community events. The South Fork River runs just blocks away, offering kayaking and fishing access.

For buyers interested in the lifestyle that comes with a walkable, historic neighborhood, this area deserves a serious look. 2. McLean — One of Belmont’s Premier Master-Planned Communities

McLean is widely regarded as one of the finest neighborhoods in Belmont. This master-planned community was developed with a clear vision: traditional neighborhood design, walkable streets, parks, and architectural variety that creates real curb appeal from block to block.

Homes in McLean range from townhomes and single-family cottages to larger custom builds, with prices generally starting around $350,000 and rising past $700,000 for premium lots and larger floor plans. The community features a pool, clubhouse, playground, and a network of walking trails that connect residents to nearby amenities.

McLean’s design is deliberately community-oriented. Front porches are standard, alleyway garages keep streets uncluttered, and pocket parks are scattered throughout the development. It is the kind of neighborhood where neighbors actually know each other, which is increasingly rare.

Because McLean attracts significant buyer demand, homes here tend to move quickly. If you are a buyer competing in this price range, having pre-approval in place and a real estate agent who can move fast is critical. 3. Catawba Heights — Historic Charm and River Access

Catawba Heights is one of Belmont’s older and more established neighborhoods, offering a mix of mid-century ranch homes, updated bungalows, and a handful of newer construction properties. The neighborhood is located along the South Fork Catawba River and offers some of the best outdoor access of any community in the city.

Home prices in Catawba Heights tend to be more attainable than in newer master-planned communities, with many homes available in the $220,000 to $400,000 range. This makes the neighborhood particularly attractive to first-time homebuyers and buyers who want more bang for their budget.

The area has a strong neighborhood association and a genuinely community-minded culture. Long-time residents take pride in the area, and the influx of buyers from Charlotte has brought new energy and investment without erasing the neighborhood’s character.

4. Reflection Pointe — Waterfront Living at Its Best

If lake access is on your wish list, Reflection Pointe is the neighborhood that deserves the top of your list. This lakefront community sits along Lake Wylie and offers a lifestyle that most people only dream about — private boat slips, a community marina, resort-style amenities, and stunning water views from some of the best homes in Gaston County.

Properties in Reflection Pointe range from non-waterfront homes starting around $450,000 to lakefront properties that can reach $1.2 million and above. The community features a clubhouse, resort pool, tennis courts, fitness center, a marina with boat storage, and a social calendar that keeps residents connected year-round.

Buyers relocating from larger metros often find that Reflection Pointe offers a caliber of lifestyle they could not achieve at the same price in their previous city. The combination of waterfront access, quality construction, and the quiet of a lake community — all within 20 minutes of Charlotte — is genuinely hard to match.

For buyers interested in luxury homes in Belmont, NC, particularly those with water access, reaching out to the Ally Bohanan Real Estate team for a private consultation is the right first step. 5. Abbey Glen and Cramerton Vicinity

The area surrounding Abbey Glen and the nearby Cramerton community offers a quieter, more spread-out feel than some of Belmont’s denser neighborhoods. Lots tend to be larger, homes often have more square footage for the price, and the pace of life feels genuinely relaxed.

This part of the Belmont market attracts families who want room to breathe — space for a backyard, a garage, and a neighborhood where kids can ride bikes and play outside without the density of a tighter urban community. Home prices in this corridor typically range from $280,000 to $550,000 depending on lot size, age, and finish level.

Cramerton itself is a small town adjacent to Belmont that shares many of the same qualities — great greenway access along the South Fork River, strong community events, and a growing number of local businesses that are making the area a destination rather than just a pass-through.

For buyers who want more space without sacrificing access to Belmont’s amenities, this part of the market is worth exploring carefully. Ally Bohanan Real Estate serves this area and can help you understand the nuances between specific subdivisions, lot values, and what to look for when comparing properties in this price range.

6. New Construction Communities in Belmont, NC

One of the most notable trends in Belmont’s real estate market over the past few years has been the growth of new construction. Several builders have introduced communities throughout the city, offering buyers the appeal of a brand-new home with modern floor plans, energy efficiency, and builder warranties.

New construction in Belmont currently spans a wide range — from entry-level townhomes in the $280,000 to $350,000 range to custom luxury single-family homes above $800,000. The appeal of new construction is obvious: no deferred maintenance, modern kitchens and bathrooms, open floor plans, and the ability to make selections that personalize your home.

7. What to Consider When Choosing a Neighborhood in Belmont

Every buyer’s priorities are different. Here are the key factors worth thinking through as you narrow down which Belmont neighborhood is the right fit for you:

Commute and Connectivity. Most Belmont neighborhoods connect to I-85 fairly quickly, but some require navigating local roads first. Think through your daily commute and how much time you are willing to spend in the car each day.

School Zones. Gaston County Schools serves Belmont, and different neighborhoods feed into different elementary, middle, and high schools. If school quality is a priority, ask your agent to walk you through the specific school assignments for each neighborhood you are considering.

HOA Fees and Community Rules. Master-planned communities like McLean and Reflection Pointe have HOA fees that cover amenities and community maintenance. Make sure you understand what is included, what the rules are, and how the HOA is managed before committing.

Long-Term Value. Some neighborhoods in Belmont have seen faster appreciation than others due to proximity to amenities, school zones, and planned commercial development. A knowledgeable local agent can help you identify which areas are positioned for continued growth.

Your Lifestyle. A waterfront community is perfect for buyers who want lake access, but it may not be the right fit for someone who wants a walkable lifestyle with restaurants and shops nearby. Match the neighborhood to how you actually want to live.

Straight answers

Frequently asked questions

What is the average home price in Belmont, NC?

Home prices in Belmont, NC, vary widely by neighborhood, size, and condition. In 2026, buyers can find homes starting in the low $200,000s in more established neighborhoods and reaching well above $1 million in waterfront communities like Reflection Pointe.

Is Belmont, NC a good place to raise a family?

Absolutely. Belmont consistently ranks as one of the most family-friendly communities in Gaston County. The city offers strong schools, numerous parks and greenways, a safe and walkable downtown, and a community culture that prioritizes families and quality of life.

How far is Belmont, NC from Charlotte?

Belmont is approximately 15 to 20 minutes from uptown Charlotte via I-85, making it one of the closest and most convenient Charlotte suburbs for daily commuters. The drive is straightforward and avoids the heaviest Charlotte traffic corridors.

Are there waterfront homes available in Belmont, NC?

Yes. Reflection Pointe is the most prominent lakefront community in Belmont, offering both waterfront and non-waterfront homes along Lake Wylie. There are also properties near the South Fork Catawba River in other parts of the city. A knowledgeable local agent can help you identify all available waterfront options in your budget.

What are the best neighborhoods for first-time homebuyers in Belmont, NC?

Catawba Heights, the Cramerton area, and certain sections of the city near downtown offer some of the most attainable price points for first-time buyers. New construction townhomes are also worth considering for buyers who want a move-in-ready home without the uncertainty of an older property.

Do I need a real estate agent to buy a home in Belmont, NC?

While you are not legally required to have an agent, working without one — particularly in a competitive market — puts you at a significant disadvantage. The seller’s agent represents the seller, not you. Having a dedicated buyer agent from a team like Ally Bohanan Real Estate costs you nothing as a buyer (the seller typically pays agent commissions) and gives you professional advocacy, market knowledge, and negotiating expertise throughout the entire process.

How do I find out what my budget should be before I start looking?

The best starting point is getting pre-approved for a mortgage. Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow, what your rate will likely be, and positions you to make an offer the moment you find the right home. The Ally Bohanan Real Estate preferred lender can walk you through this process quickly and without pressure.

Are there new construction homes available in Belmont, NC?

Yes, Belmont has seen significant new construction activity in recent years. Multiple builders are active in the market, offering everything from entry-level townhomes to custom luxury builds. Ally Bohanan Real Estate has experience guiding buyers through new construction purchases and can also facilitate fully custom home builds through their partnership with Bohanan Construction.

What should I look for when comparing neighborhoods in Belmont, NC?

Key factors include school zone assignments, HOA fees and rules, proximity to I-85 and Charlotte, neighborhood amenities, lot sizes, and the community’s overall character. Every buyer has different priorities, and a local agent can help you weigh these factors against your specific lifestyle and budget.
