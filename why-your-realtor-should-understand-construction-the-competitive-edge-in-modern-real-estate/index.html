Why Your Realtor Should Understand Construction: The Competitive Edge in Modern Real Estate

By Ally BohananPublished April 30, 20265 min read

Building

The short answer

In the modern real estate market, the glossy photos on a listing site are designed to do one thing: entice you. High-end finishes, fresh coats of paint, and professional staging can make almost any house look like a dream home.

In the modern real estate market, the glossy photos on a listing site are designed to do one thing: entice you. High-end finishes, fresh coats of paint, and professional staging can make almost any house look like a dream home. However, for most buyers, a home is the largest financial investment they will ever make. When the stakes are this high, “aesthetic appeal” isn’t enough. You need to know what is happening behind the drywall, under the floorboards, and inside the crawlspace.

This is where the value of a realtor with a deep background in construction—like the expertise offered by Ally Bohanan and her partnership with Bohanan Construction—becomes a game-changer. Most agents are trained in sales, contracts, and marketing. While those skills are vital, they often stop at the surface. A realtor who understands construction looks at a house through a different lens, acting as a first line of defense against “money pits” and as a visionary for untapped potential.

Here is an in-depth look at why having a construction-literate realtor is the single most important advantage you can have in today’s market.

1. Seeing Through the “Flip”: The Danger of Cosmetic Cover-ups

We live in the era of the “fast flip.” Investors often buy distressed properties, apply quick cosmetic fixes, and put them back on the market for a significant profit. While many flippers do excellent, high-quality work, others prioritize speed and profit margins over structural integrity.

A standard realtor might see a beautifully renovated kitchen with quartz countertops and stainless steel appliances. A realtor with a construction background, however, will look at the cabinet alignment to see if the floors are sagging. They will check the electrical panel to see if it was upgraded to handle those new appliances, or if the flipper simply “piggybacked” off old, dangerous wiring.

The Red Flags Only an Expert Sees:

  • Fresh Paint in Specific Spots: Is it a design choice, or is it covering up a recurring water stain from a leaky roof?

  • New Flooring Over Old: Is that luxury vinyl plank hiding a cracked slab or uneven subflooring that will cause squeaks and failures in six months?

  • Incomplete Permitting: A construction-savvy realtor knows which projects require permits. If they see a finished basement but no record of an electrical permit, they know you’re walking into a potential legal and safety nightmare.

2. Accurate Budgeting for Renovations and Repairs

One of the most stressful parts of buying a home that isn’t “perfect” is the unknown cost of repairs. When a buyer asks, “How much would it cost to knock down this wall?” or “What will it take to fix this deck?”, a typical agent might give a vague estimate or tell you to call a contractor later.

A realtor who understands the “bones” of a building can provide a much more accurate “ballpark” figure on the spot. Because they understand labor costs, material prices, and structural requirements, they can help you determine if a $350,000 home with $50,000 in needed work is a better deal than a $425,000 “turnkey” property.

Value-Added Insights:

  • Load-Bearing vs. Partition Walls: They can often identify which walls are structural, saving you from dreaming of an open-concept layout that would actually cost $20,000 in steel beams to execute.

  • Mechanical Lifespans: They can evaluate the age and condition of the HVAC, water heater, and roof, helping you build a 5-year financial plan for the home.

3. Navigating the Inspection Process with Confidence

The home inspection is often the most nerve-wracking part of a real estate transaction. The inspector’s report can be 50 pages long, filled with terrifying-language about “moisture intrusion,” “foundation settlement,” and “code violations.”

A realtor without construction knowledge might get spooked by a minor issue or, conversely, fail to realize how serious a “small” note actually is. A construction-savvy agent acts as a translator. They can look at an inspection report and say, “The inspector mentioned the grading is off. That’s a weekend of shoveling and $200 in dirt, not a foundation failure. Let’s not lose the house over it.” Conversely, they can spot a “small crack” in a basement wall and recognize it as a sign of hydrostatic pressure that requires a $15,000 drainage system.

4. The Seller’s Advantage: Pre-Listing Optimization

If you are selling a home, a realtor with construction insight is an even greater asset. Before the house ever hits the market, they can walk through your property and identify the “high-ROI” (Return on Investment) fixes.

Not every repair is worth the money. You might think you need to renovate the entire master bathroom to sell, but your realtor might suggest that simply replacing the vanity, lighting, and regrouting the tile can deliver much of the impact for a fraction of the cost.

The Bohanan Advantage:

By leveraging a partnership with a construction firm, a realtor can help sellers streamline the “Fix-to-Sell” process. They can manage the subcontractors, ensure the work is done to code, and make sure the home passes the buyer’s inspection with flying colors. This reduces the “days on market” and often leads to much higher offers.

5. Identifying “Diamonds in the Rough”

In a competitive market, everyone is fighting over the houses that are already perfect. This leads to bidding wars and overpaying. The real “wealth-building” in real estate happens when you find a house that looks unappealing to the average buyer but has “good bones.”

A construction-literate realtor can spot these opportunities. They see the 1970s ranch with the avocado-green appliances and see a solid foundation, a functional layout, and high-quality cedar siding. They can help you envision the transformation, knowing that the “ugly” parts are purely cosmetic and easily fixed.

6. Communication with Contractors

Once you buy a home, the relationship with your realtor usually ends. But a realtor tied into the construction world becomes a long-term resource. If you want to build an addition three years later, you have a direct line to someone who understands the local building codes, knows which contractors are reliable, and understands how that addition will affect your home’s future resale value.

Conclusion: Expertise You Can Build On

Real estate is about more than just finding a place to sleep; it’s about building equity and ensuring safety. When you work with a professional who understands both the market trends and the physical reality of construction, you are protected.

Ally Bohanan’s background creates a synergy that few other agents can match. Whether you are a first-time buyer trying to avoid a mistake, or a seasoned investor looking for a property with structural upside, the “construction lens” is your most valuable asset. Don’t just buy a house based on the color of the walls—buy a house based on the strength of its foundation and the expertise of the person standing beside you.
