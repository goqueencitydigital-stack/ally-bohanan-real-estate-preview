The short answer

Selling a home in Gastonia or anywhere in Gaston County usually starts six to eight weeks before the list date with a pricing consultation, then repairs, staging and photography. Once a buyer’s offer is accepted, North Carolina’s standard contract gives the buyer a negotiated due diligence period for inspections and financing, and a closing attorney handles the closing. In North Carolina the seller must give the buyer a Residential Property and Owners’ Association Disclosure Statement no later than the time the buyer makes an offer.

Seller preparation guide

How to prepare your home to sell

Most of what makes a listing succeed happens before it goes live. This is the order Ally recommends. The timeline tool above turns it into a week-by-week plan.

1. Start with a pricing conversation, not a number

Ally walks through the home, studies recent nearby sales and current competition, and explains the range she would list in and why. Online estimates can be a starting point, but they cannot see your kitchen, your lot or the street. That is why Ally prices homes in person.

2. Gather your documents early

Mortgage payoff, HOA contacts, warranties, permits, surveys and utility averages. Buyers ask for them, and having them ready keeps the contract moving.

3. Repair what buyers will notice

Ally’s construction background helps her separate the repairs that pay for themselves from the ones buyers will overlook. A pre-listing inspection can surface surprises while you still have time to choose.

4. Declutter, then stage

Pack what you will not need for a few months. Then stage the rooms that matter most: the living room, kitchen and primary bedroom. See home staging.

5. Prepare for photography

Deep clean, open the blinds, turn every light on, clear the counters and move the cars. Photography day sets the first impression for most buyers.

6. Plan for showings

Agree on notice times, a pet plan and lockbox access before launch, so the first weekend goes smoothly.

Disclosures

What paperwork does a North Carolina seller need?

North Carolina puts more paperwork on sellers up front than many people expect. Before a buyer makes an offer, you must hand over two state forms from the North Carolina Real Estate Commission:

  • Residential Property and Owners’ Association Disclosure Statement. Questions about the water and sewer or septic system, roof and structure, plumbing, electrical and HVAC, wood-destroying insects, zoning and covenants, environmental issues and any owners’ association. You can answer No Representation, but you must answer every question.
  • Mineral and Oil and Gas Rights Mandatory Disclosure Statement. Whether those rights have been severed from the property.

Before Ally can market your home, the firm must have a written listing agreement with you, and she will review NCREC’s Working With Real Estate Agents Disclosure with you at the first substantial conversation. Gather your survey, HOA documents, warranties, permits for past work and recent utility bills at the same time. If the home has a private well or septic system, find the septic permit and any pumping or repair records; Gaston County Environmental Health keeps permit files.

Under contract

What happens between the offer and the closing?

Most Gaston County sales use the standard North Carolina Offer to Purchase and Contract. The parts sellers negotiate are the price, the due diligence fee and period, the earnest money, the closing date and possession.

  • Due diligence period. The buyer inspects, appraises, surveys and finalizes the loan. The buyer can walk away for any reason before it ends; you cannot.
  • Repair requests. Usually arrive near the end of due diligence. Ally’s construction background helps here: she can tell you which items are real, what they are likely to cost and whether a credit makes more sense than a repair.
  • Appraisal. If the buyer is financing, the lender’s appraiser checks the price. Pricing with the appraisal in mind avoids renegotiation later.
  • Closing. In North Carolina a closing attorney handles the closing and records the deed with the Gaston County Register of Deeds.

Costs

What does it cost to sell?

Ally walks every seller through an estimated net sheet before listing. The categories are the same for most sales:

  • Your mortgage payoff and any home equity line.
  • North Carolina excise tax on the deed: $1 for each $500 of the sale price (G.S. 105-228.30), paid by the seller to the register of deeds. On a $400,000 sale that is $800.
  • Prorated property taxes for the part of the year you owned the home. Gaston County and city rates are published by the county.
  • Repairs or credits you agree to after inspections.
  • Listing compensation, which NCREC states is negotiable and must be written into the listing agreement.

In South Carolina the deed recording fee is $1.85 for each $500 of value instead of North Carolina’s excise tax. Your closing attorney prepares the final numbers.

Straight answers

Selling questions

How long does it take to sell a house in Gastonia?

Plan on two clocks. Preparation usually takes a few weeks to two months, depending on repairs and staging. After you accept an offer, the contract sets the due diligence period and closing date that you and the buyer negotiate, so the calendar from contract to closing is agreed in writing up front. How fast a home goes under contract depends on price, condition and the competition that month, which is why Ally prices in person rather than promising a number of days.

What do I have to disclose when I sell in North Carolina?

Under N.C.G.S. 47E, owners of most one- to four-unit homes must complete the NCREC Residential Property and Owners’ Association Disclosure Statement and the Mineral and Oil and Gas Rights Mandatory Disclosure Statement, and give them to the buyer no later than the time the buyer makes an offer. You answer each question Yes, No, No Representation or Not Applicable. If the buyer does not get the forms in time, the buyer may be able to cancel within three calendar days. Some transfers, such as never-occupied new construction, are exempt; ask an attorney if you think one applies.

What does the due diligence fee mean for a seller?

Under the standard North Carolina Offer to Purchase and Contract, the due diligence fee is paid directly to the seller and is usually credited to the buyer at closing. During the due diligence period the buyer can terminate for any reason, while the seller stays bound. If the buyer terminates in time, the buyer gets the earnest money back but not the due diligence fee, unless the seller breached the contract. NCREC notes that neither fee is required by law; both are negotiated.

What does it cost to sell a house in North Carolina?

Common seller costs are the mortgage payoff, the state excise tax on the deed ($1 for each $500 of the price, paid to the county register of deeds), prorated property taxes, any repairs or credits you agree to, and the compensation set out in your listing agreement. NCREC is clear that agent compensation is negotiable and must be stated in the written listing agreement. Your closing attorney prepares the final settlement statement.

Is selling in York County, SC different?

Yes, in a few ways. South Carolina sellers use the SC Real Estate Commission’s Residential Property Condition Disclosure Statement, delivered before the contract is signed unless the contract says otherwise. Instead of North Carolina’s excise tax, South Carolina charges a deed recording fee of $1.85 for each $500 of value, generally owed by the seller. Ally is licensed in both states and lists homes in Lake Wylie, Clover, York, Rock Hill and Fort Mill.

My home was built before 1978. Is there anything extra?

Yes. Federal rules require sellers of most pre-1978 housing to give buyers the EPA pamphlet “Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home,” disclose any known lead-based paint information and records, and allow the buyer a 10-day period for a lead inspection unless the buyer waives it. Older homes in historic neighborhoods and mill villages around Gastonia, Belmont and Mount Holly often fall into this group, so check the year built early.

Should I sell first or buy first?

It depends on your equity, your loan options and how much overlap you can carry. Some sellers make the purchase contingent on their sale, some negotiate a later closing or a short rent-back, and some use bridge financing. Ally plans both sides together and brings in a lender early so you know which options are real before you list.
